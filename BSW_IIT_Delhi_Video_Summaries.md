## 1. BSW CV Making Session | Core (w/ Deep Bansal)

**Published:** 26 December 2024 · **Length:** 38:55  
**Watch:** [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6pGp5EVT1ZQ)  
**Source note:** Limited captions.

### Summary

A hands-on session for students preparing a core-sector internship CV. It frames the CV as a shortlisting document that should make technical relevance obvious: the reader should quickly see the candidate's academic base, domain exposure, projects, tools, and evidence of execution. The session is best read alongside Anup Kulkarni's companion core-CV session below.

### Key points

- Tailor the CV to the core role instead of sending a generic profile everywhere.
- Prioritize relevant coursework, laboratory work, technical projects, research, and engineering tools.
- Explain each project through the problem, your individual contribution, the method or tool used, and the result.
- Use numbers where defensible: performance gains, scale, accuracy, cost, time, or throughput.
- Do not panic if you lack a first-year internship; strong projects and academic work can fill that gap.
- Keep formatting clean, consistent, concise, and easy to scan.

## 2. BSW CV Making Session | Core (w/ Anup Kulkarni)

**Published:** 26 December 2024 · **Length:** 1:00:33  
**Watch:** [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cRCx5pUmRQQ)

### Summary

Anup Kulkarni explains why companies use CVs for shortlisting and walks section-by-section through a core CV. He covers academic details, scholastic achievements, internships, projects, positions of responsibility, and extracurriculars, with emphasis on presenting technical work clearly and truthfully.

### Key points

- A typical core CV order is: academics, scholastic achievements, internships, projects, PoRs, and ECAs.
- Four or five strong scholastic points are better than padding; ask a professor before claiming course-rank or recommendation-based achievements.
- A prior internship is useful but not mandatory after first year.
- For a project or research internship, state the title, supervisor/organization, duration, objective, your work, tools, and outcome.
- Make ownership explicit when a project was completed in a team.
- Prefer concrete technical language over vague claims such as “worked on” or “helped with.”
- Expect the CV to guide interview questions, so be ready to defend every line.

## 3. BSW CV Making Session | Consult (w/ Vinamra Bedia)

**Published:** 26 December 2024 · **Length:** 55:21  
**Watch:** [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ssa7AXMpmXs)

### Summary

This session explains the “spike” or “peak” model used to think about consulting CVs. Recruiters look across scholastics, internships, positions of responsibility, and extracurricular achievements for strong evidence of academic ability, leadership, initiative, and impact—not just participation.

### Key points

- The four major areas are scholastics, internships, PoRs, and ECAs; aim for multiple genuinely strong sections.
- Recent institute-level academic achievements usually carry more weight than routine school-era results.
- Avoid low-information filler such as JEE rank when the institute/branch already conveys it.
- Branded corporate internships, strong foreign research experiences, SURA, and substantive startup work can strengthen the internship section.
- A consulting CV should show leadership and a coherent story across activities, not a random collection of titles.
- External competitions and institute representation often demonstrate more selectivity than internal participation.
- Quantify impact—money, growth, participation, efficiency, reach, rankings, or wins—where the figure is real and explainable.
- Balance the page; two well-explained internships are generally better than a crowded list.

## 5. BSW CV Making Session | SDE, Quant, Analytics (w/ Chaitanya Mittal)

**Published:** 26 December 2024 · **Length:** 31:01  
**Watch:** [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4HDJX0zuycs)

### Summary

Chaitanya Mittal reviews the structure and presentation of technical CVs for software, quantitative, and analytics roles. The session emphasizes projects, internships, technical skills, measurable outcomes, and meticulous formatting.

### Key points

- Core sections include scholastics, projects, technical skills, PoRs, and ECAs; add internships when applicable.
- Projects and internships should dominate a technical CV because they demonstrate applied ability.
- Quantify outcomes such as accuracy, false-positive rate, speed, revenue, or efficiency.

## 7. Analytics Intern FAQs

**Published:** 26 December 2024 · **Length:** 3:22  
**Watch:** [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ooc-1_4D3y0)

### Summary

A rapid overview of analytics internships: what analysts do, which skills matter, how much coding is expected, useful courses, suitable second-year experiences, and the relative importance of CV components.

### Key points

- Analysts collect, clean, process, visualize, and statistically interpret data to understand or forecast trends.
- Statistics, probability, puzzles, DSA, SQL, and DBMS are useful foundations; R can be a useful additional analytics tool.

- A varied project portfolio—ML, DSA, and SQL/data work—is preferable.
- CGPA cutoffs differ by company and can be 8 or 8.5; ECAs and PoRs are useful but usually secondary for analyst roles.

## 9. Consult Intern FAQs

**Published:** 26 December 2024 · **Length:** 18:28  
**Watch:** [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GyVqlN3l1e0)

### Summary

A detailed consulting-internship primer covering the nature of consulting work, the selection funnel, CV spikes, buddy cases, preparation groups, and interview-day conduct.

### Key points

- Consulting combines structured problem solving, research, quantitative analysis, stakeholder conversations, and concise presentation.
- The usual funnel is CV shortlisting, buddy/case rounds, and final interviews; CV shortlisting is often the largest bottleneck.
- The five discussed CV areas are CGPA, scholastics, internships, PoRs, and ECAs; firms seek several strong sections or “spikes.”
- A CGPA above 8 is described as helpful, with 8.5–9 often becoming a stronger academic signal; other exceptional sections can partly compensate.

- Treat buddy cases seriously, communicate scheduling conflicts early, seek varied feedback, and practice with more than one person.

## 10. Quant Intern FAQs

**Published:** 26 December 2024 · **Length:** 6:35  
**Watch:** [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lOMgNxnu2Ig)

### Summary

A concise roadmap for quantitative internship preparation, covering company types, branch restrictions, programming, probability, tests, mental math, market making, and the value of prior projects.

### Key points

- Opportunities include bank quant roles and specialist trading/HFT firms; branch eligibility is company-specific.
- Start competitive programming early and become comfortable with C++, DSA, dynamic programming, and graphs.
- Finance and ML are not universal prerequisites, though some firms test them and ML can strengthen a CV.
- Shortlisting often values CGPA, scholastics, olympiad-style achievements, and relevant company competitions.
- Probability, puzzles, game theory, and time-bound problem solving matter more than chasing a rating alone; 1500–1700 Codeforces is offered as a useful—not mandatory—benchmark.
- Do company-specific mental-math and market-making practice close to the tests rather than spending months on it too early.
- Understand bid, ask, midpoint, estimation, and how to communicate assumptions in a market-making exercise.
- Theory-intensive AI/ML/TCS projects help, but productive coding preparation can also be a valid use of breaks.

## 11. SDE Intern FAQs

**Published:** 26 December 2024 · **Length:** 7:52  
**Watch:** [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=33XDA8LPPOA)

### Summary

An SDE internship roadmap covering programming language, ML, competitive programming, LeetCode, projects, interview communication, and introductory systems knowledge.

### Key points

- Any language can work initially, but C++ is recommended before interviews because some assessments probe C++ concepts.
- ML is generally tested only when listed in a project, except for some quant-development roles where basic ML may be directly relevant.
- Start CP as early as practical, focus on diverse problems, and review editorials and unsolved questions after contests.
- Use LeetCode for standard DSA patterns and CP/virtual contests for speed and broader problem-solving exposure.

- Write clean code with meaningful names; study systems only after becoming confident in DSA and algorithms.

## 13. A Message from Counselling Services on World Suicide Prevention Day 2022

**Published:** 10 September 2022 · **Length:** 11:28  
**Watch:** [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vACFGX9YAFg)

### Summary

IIT Delhi Counselling Services explains suicide risk factors and warning signs, corrects common misconceptions, and gives practical guidance for supporting someone who may be suicidal. The central message is that suicide is preventable and direct, compassionate action can save a life.

### Key points

- Warning signs can include hopelessness, talking about death, making a plan, feeling trapped or burdensome, withdrawal, severe mood changes, substance misuse, giving possessions away, or saying goodbye.
- Asking directly about suicidal thoughts does not “plant the idea”; it can open a path to help.
- Listen without judgment, do not lecture or minimize, and do not promise secrecy.
- If risk appears immediate, do not leave the person alone; reduce access to means and contact trusted staff, security, counsellors, or emergency support.
- Professional treatment and social support can help; the person is not alone.
- Maintain everyday mental health through connection, help-seeking, rest, and timely professional care.
- The video's information is educational, not a substitute for assessment by a qualified mental-health professional.

## Cross-video takeaways

- **Build evidence, not labels.** Whether applying for core, consulting, analytics, quant, or SDE roles, projects and achievements should show what you did and what changed.
- **Start broad, then specialize.** Early college is for exploration; sustained work later creates a visible spike.
- **Technical foundations compound.** Probability, DSA, programming, statistics, and structured problem solving recur across several career tracks.
- **Communication is part of competence.** Clear CV bullets, interview narration, stakeholder listening, and concise presentation repeatedly distinguish strong candidates.
- **Relationships matter.** Faculty mentors, seniors, peers, alumni, buddies, and teammates open learning and opportunity—when approached genuinely.
- **Your branch is not your ceiling.** Several alumni moved from non-circuital engineering into technology, economics, consulting, and entrepreneurship.
- **Career success is not purely individual.** Diverse teams, mental-health support, inclusive institutions, and strong networks make ambitious work more sustainable.
